#### Recap
I used the initial lab credentials to review the following Active Directory delegations in BloodHound.
1. WriteSPN exposed a target principal to Kerberoasting.
2. AddSelf exposed a group to unauthorized self-enrollment.
3. ReadGMSAPassword exposed the managed password for ansible_dev$.
4. ForceChangePassword exposed another account to an unauthorized password reset.
5. WriteOwner exposed control of another account's ACL. That account belonged to Remote Management Users.
The delegation chain led to access as John, and the WinRM session exposed the user flag.

The RBCD attempt failed because John lacked write access to the DC01 delegation settings. The account also found no vulnerable certificate template. The AD Recycle Bin exposed a deleted CA operator account that could be restored and reset, although the exact basis for the effective reset permission was not confirmed. The restored account exposed an ESC15 template. Certificate-based administrator access then led to elevated WinRM access and the root flag.


Assessment notes
1. Use BloodHound to trace effective access across multiple directory delegations.
2. When testing disproves the assumptions behind an elevation path, reassess the evidence and move to the next credible lead.

Key Concepts:
1. Audit certificate templates for ESC15 and review which principals can enroll in them.
